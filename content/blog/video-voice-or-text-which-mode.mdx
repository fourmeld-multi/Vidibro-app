Most Omegle-style platforms give you exactly one option: video, camera on, or nothing. Vidibro splits that into three genuinely separate modes, and which one to pick isn't obvious unless you know what each one is actually good for.

## Video: for when you want the whole conversation

Video carries information the other two modes can't — tone through expression, timing through pauses you can see coming, the small human details a voice or text conversation misses entirely. It's also the highest-commitment option: camera on means you're visible, which changes how a conversation feels for a lot of people, in both directions.

**Best for:** when you have the time and energy for a full conversation, and when you'd rather read someone's reaction directly than infer it.

## Voice-only: video's upside without the camera

Voice-only chat keeps the real-time, unscripted feel of a live conversation — you hear tone, timing, hesitation — without requiring you to be on camera. That matters more than it sounds like it would: plenty of people are more willing to be genuinely candid when they're not worried about how they look while saying it.

**Best for:** conversations where you want spontaneity and real tone, but the camera itself would make you (or the other person) more guarded than you want to be. It's also the practical choice if you're somewhere you can talk but shouldn't be on video — commuting, in bed, at 1am without turning a light on.

## Text: the lowest-stakes option, and underrated for it

Text chat removes the pressure of a live response entirely — you can think before you send, and so can they. It's also the most accessible mode by a wide margin: no camera, no microphone, works anywhere you can type. For a lot of people, especially anyone dealing with anxiety around live conversation, text is where the best exchanges actually happen, precisely because there's no pressure to respond instantly.

**Best for:** when you want to ease into a conversation, when you're somewhere you can't use audio or video at all, or when you'd simply rather write than talk out loud.

## Accessibility is a real reason to have three modes, not a footnote

Having genuinely separate modes isn't just about mood — it changes who can actually use the product comfortably. Someone who's deaf or hard of hearing can have a full, real conversation over text without video's audio dependency getting in the way. Someone with low vision can use voice chat without needing to track a video feed at all. A single video-only option quietly excludes both of those situations by default; splitting video, voice, and text into real, separate modes — not a video call with the camera just turned off — means each one is actually built to stand on its own rather than being a degraded version of video chat.

## There's no wrong choice — and you can always switch

None of these modes is the "real" way to use Vidibro — they're three different tools for three different moods. If video feels like too much right now, voice or text aren't a downgrade; they're just a better fit for the moment. And if a text conversation is going somewhere you'd rather take further, nothing stops you from starting a new video or voice match instead.
