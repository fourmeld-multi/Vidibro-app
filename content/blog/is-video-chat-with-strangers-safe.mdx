Anonymous, unmoderated-by-default chat with strangers carries real risk — pretending otherwise would be dishonest, and so would pretending it's uniquely dangerous compared to the rest of the internet. Here's an honest breakdown.

## What's actually structurally safe about it

Vidibro's calls run peer-to-peer over WebRTC — your video and audio stream directly to the other person's browser, not through a company server. There's nothing to store because nothing passes through a place where it could be stored. No account means no persistent profile tied to your identity for someone to dig into later. That's a real, structural privacy advantage over platforms that route your call through their own infrastructure.

## What it doesn't do

There's no proactive scanning of video or audio content before it reaches you — nothing is watching the call in real time to flag it. If you're matched with someone behaving badly, hitting **Next** immediately disconnects you and moves you to a new match; that's a real, working tool, and it's the main lever you have in the moment. Treat it as your first move, not a last resort.

## Red flags worth actually watching for

- **Immediate pressure to move the conversation to another app.** This is a well-documented pattern in online scams and sextortion — moving off-platform removes whatever record exists of what was said, and it's a request that has no good reason behind it in a genuinely casual chat.
- **Any request for money, gift cards, or financial information.** No legitimate reason exists for a stranger on a random chat platform to need this from you, ever.
- **Anyone asking you to do something on camera "just this once" that you wouldn't otherwise.** The pressure itself is the tell — a genuine conversation doesn't need you to prove anything.
- **Someone who already seems to know details about you that you didn't share.** Rare, but worth disconnecting from immediately if it happens.

## Habits that actually reduce risk

Keep identifying details — your school, workplace, exact location, phone number — out of a first conversation with a stranger, the same way you would with anyone you just met anywhere else. Trust the moment you feel uncomfortable enough to want to leave; that instinct is usually right, and acting on it costs nothing. And remember that anonymity runs both ways — you don't owe a stranger justification for disconnecting.

## A note on screen recording

Peer-to-peer architecture protects where your call travels, but it can't stop the person on the other end from recording their own screen — no video platform, P2P or server-relayed, can prevent that on a technical level. This isn't a Vidibro-specific limitation; it's true of every video call anywhere. The practical response is the same as with any of the red flags above: if a conversation makes you uncomfortable enough to wonder about this, that discomfort is itself the signal to disconnect, not something to reason your way past.

## The honest summary

Random video chat isn't inherently dangerous, but it is genuinely unmoderated by default — the platform's job is to get you connected instantly and give you fast, working tools to leave a bad match, not to police every conversation before it happens. Understanding that trade-off, rather than assuming either "it's totally safe" or "it's obviously reckless," is what actually keeps the experience good.
